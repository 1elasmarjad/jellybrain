# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Jellybrain is for solo founders and small startup teams who need one reliable place to see what needs attention. They are moving quickly across email, meetings, Slack, outbound, hiring, fundraising, customer work, and internal docs. They need a shared operational dashboard that keeps daily priorities, overdue work, promised follow-ups, and owner accountability visible without forcing them to manually check every tool.

## Product Purpose

Jellybrain is a lightweight AI co-founder and chief-of-staff for startup tasks, reminders, and follow-ups. The MVP is a shared company task tracker with owners, due dates, priorities, statuses, company context, team dashboard views, and a daily reminder digest.

The product exists to help founders and startup teams notice what matters, remember what was promised, and make sure important follow-ups do not get missed. Success means the team can quickly answer: what needs attention today, what is overdue, who owns each next step, and what context is needed to act.

## Brand Personality

Clean, modern, and operationally sharp. Jellybrain should feel like a focused startup command center: calm enough for daily use, precise enough for high-stakes founder work, and lightweight enough to stay out of the way.

The product can use AI behind the scenes, but the interface should not feel overly AI-branded, chat-first, gimmicky, or cute. The experience should emphasize clear dashboards, trustworthy reminders, and fast action.

## Anti-references

Avoid interfaces that feel too AI-ey, mascot-driven, prompt-centric, or novelty-oriented. Avoid generic SaaS dashboard sameness, cluttered CRM density, childish task-app styling, and heavy enterprise software patterns that slow people down.

Do not make every screen feel like a landing page. Product screens should prioritize scanning, ownership, status, deadlines, and quick task completion over decorative sections or oversized marketing composition.

## Design Principles

1. Make attention obvious: overdue, due today, blocked, and high-priority work should be easy to identify at a glance.
2. Keep founders in flow: common actions like creating, assigning, completing, and reviewing tasks should be direct and low-friction.
3. Show context where it helps: attach people, companies, sources, and notes to tasks without burying the task itself.
4. AI should feel practical, not performative: use intelligence to suggest, remind, summarize, and surface risks while keeping the interface grounded in concrete work.
5. Build reusable product primitives: buttons, inputs, badges, cards, lists, dialogs, and form controls should come from shared components so the app stays consistent as it grows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard navigation, focus states, and form labeling. Support reduced motion preferences for animated or transitional UI. Do not rely on color alone to communicate priority, status, overdue state, or destructive actions.
